Introduction

A clinical start, a commercial mindset.

I trained as a nurse (BSc in Nursing), and that is where I learned to listen closely, decide what needs attention first and explain things clearly to people who are worried. I carried those habits into virtual healthcare support, e-commerce operations, customer success and B2B outreach.

What connects all of it is simple. People stay with a business when they feel understood, when getting started is easy and when someone notices a problem before they have to report it. That is the work I want to do full time.

Value proposition

The customer problems I help a team solve.

Most growing companies don't have a product problem. They have a gap between what customers were promised and what happens in the weeks after they sign.

Slow starts

Customers who never quite get going

What I do: build a structured onboarding path with clear milestones, so every new customer knows the next step and reaches a first win quickly.

Low adoption

Customers who pay but barely use the product

What I do: find where people stall, teach the features that matter for their goals and follow up until usage becomes a habit.

Going quiet

Customers who disengage without saying why

What I do: run proactive check-ins that are useful, not routine, and log what I learn so the whole team benefits.

Silent churn

Customers who leave with no warning

What I do: keep a simple customer health view in the CRM, spot risk signals early and act while there is still time to help.

About

I make it easy for customers to succeed.

Customer Success is a business function that looks after the relationship after the sale. It is where first impressions become renewals.

My path

From the ward to the workflow.

My career started in nursing. Clinical work gave me a working method I still use: listen first, check the facts, decide what is urgent, communicate clearly and write things down so the next person knows what happened.

From there I moved into healthcare virtual assistance, supporting practices with patient-facing and administrative work for more than three years. I also worked in e-commerce operations, where a late reply or a wrong order shows up straight away in how a customer feels about a business.

I then moved into B2B work: customer success and sales support for SaaS businesses, and outreach for a US staffing company. Doing both sides of the customer conversation showed me how much of retention is decided early, by how well a customer is welcomed and how clearly they see value.

I now position myself as a Customer Success professional because it brings together what I do best: structure, empathy, follow-through and an eye on the business outcome.

How I work

Four principles.

Start with the customer's goal

A customer buys software to get something done. I ask about that outcome early and keep coming back to it.

Make the next step obvious

Confusion is the quietest reason customers drift. Clear milestones, plain language and a named owner for every step reduce it.

Notice early, act early

Fewer logins, slower replies and unanswered check-ins are signals. I record them and respond while the problem is still small.

Write it down

Good notes in the CRM mean the next conversation, with me or a colleague, starts where the last one ended.
